# Chapter 5. A/B Testing

How do we decide whether the difference between two variants, such as two web page designs, is real or only sampling noise? The chapter presents the classical frequentist toolkit for A/B testing and randomized experiments. It derives the variance of a sample mean of uncorrelated observations, defines the standard error, illustrates the central limit theorem by simulation, and develops confidence intervals, null and alternative hypotheses, the p value, the significance level, Type I and Type II errors, the z score and a five step testing procedure. Worked R examples include the Trial of the Pyx, the Mythbusters yawning experiment, Coke versus Pepsi, Avonex, Viagra, Lord Rayleigh's argon data, SimCity and Google search algorithms, with tests for proportions and a two sample t test. It then treats multiple testing, the Bonferroni correction and the false discovery rate, and discusses randomized controlled trials, field experiments, split plot designs, causation and observational data. Bayesian testing is deferred to a later chapter.

## From the literature

the central limit theorem and its origin in the De Moivre Laplace theorem (de Moivre 1738); the Galton board (Galton 1889); the Benjamini Hochberg procedure for the false discovery rate (Benjamini and Hochberg 1995); the 1948 streptomycin trial in tuberculosis, attributed by the chapter to Austin Bradford Hill; randomization, experimental design and split plot designs (Ronald Fisher and colleagues at Rothamsted); the discovery of argon (Lord Rayleigh); the five sigma criterion in the Higgs boson announcement (two teams at CERN); a diet and mammographic density study (Garcia Arenzana and coauthors 2014); a randomized field experiment on electoral fraud in Russia (Enikolopov, Korovkin, Petrova, Sonin and Zakharov 2013); a randomized experiment on environmental audits in Gujarat (Duflo, Greenstone, Pande and Ryan 2013).
