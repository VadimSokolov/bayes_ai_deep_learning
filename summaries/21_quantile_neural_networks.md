How can posteriors, predictive distributions and expected utilities be learned directly, without density estimation or an explicit likelihood? The chapter introduces quantile neural networks, which represent a conditional distribution by its quantile function and are trained with the check (pinball) loss. It opens with an inventory decision in which asymmetric costs select a quantile, then covers linear quantile regression on the mtcars data, a network that takes the quantile level as an input, a cosine embedding of that level, noncrossing constraints, a synthetic comparison of an implicit and an explicit network, and the 1 Wasserstein distance. Demand forecasting (a linear quantile baseline on simulated data, with a coverage check) follows. The chapter then turns to Bayesian inference: a generative formulation through noise outsourcing, Bayes' rule for quantiles with a normal normal example as a Wang distortion, and maximum expected utility as an integral over the quantile level, checked on a portfolio problem with exponential utility against its closed form. It closes with an optional comparison with quantile trend filtering, distributional reinforcement learning and practical checks for a learned quantile network.

## New results

The catalog records two results taken from earlier papers by Polson and coauthors: quantile networks for maximum expected utility (Polson, Ruggeri and Sokolov 2024) and the hierarchical form of quantile trend filtering (Polson and Scott 2016).

<!-- results:begin -->
- [Maximum expected utility by a quantile neural network](../RESULTS.md#meu_quantile_network_method) (Section 21.6.1, page 700; conditional on a known utility function, the ability to simulate parameters from the prior and data from the likelihood or a forward simulator, and a network that represents the quantile function of the utility; the chapter proves no approximation error, convergence rate or consistency result for the network): A network trained with the check loss on simulated utilities, with the decision and the quantile level as inputs, approximates the quantile function of the utility; integrating it over the level approximates the expected utility, and the optimal decision maximizes that integral. First stated in Polson, Ruggeri and Sokolov (2024), Generative Bayesian Computation for Maximum Expected Utility, Entropy 26(12), 1076.
- [Hierarchical model for second order trend filtering with quantile loss](../RESULTS.md#quantile_trend_filtering_hierarchical_model) (Section 21.8, page 702; conditional on the hierarchical representation, which the chapter attributes to Polson and Scott (2016) through envelope duality and does not derive, with unit likelihood scale and unpenalized level and slope, the conditions the chapter states for the posterior mode to give the penalized objective): Second order trend filtering with quantile loss has a working hierarchical model, an asymmetric Laplace likelihood with Laplace priors on second differences, whose posterior mode is the penalized estimate. First stated in Polson and Scott (2016), Mixtures, Envelopes and Hierarchical Duality, Journal of the Royal Statistical Society Series B 78(4), 701 to 727.
