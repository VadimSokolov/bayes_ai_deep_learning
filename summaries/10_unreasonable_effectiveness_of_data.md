# Chapter 10. Unreasonable Effectiveness of Data

From *Bayes, Artificial Intelligence, and Deep Learning: Foundations of Data Science* by Nick Polson and Vadim Sokolov (2026), chapter 10. This chapter opens Part II and asks when aggregating many observations or judgments yields accurate prediction, and when it entrenches error. It begins with the history of data driven estimation: the meridian arc data of Boscovich and a linear regression of arc length on the squared sine of latitude to find the shape of the Earth, the average of pairwise slopes, least absolute deviations, least squares, the weighted median and regression to the mean. A Formula One example shows real time strategy optimization from sensor data. The second half studies the wisdom and madness of crowds: speculative bubbles, Galton's ox weight contest, where the median of about 800 guesses fell within 1 percent of the true weight, and the smart money and dumb money model of learning one's type from prices as an identification problem with posterior odds and a likelihood ratio. It names the conditions that made Galton's crowd wise (independence, diversity, incentive alignment, appropriate aggregation) and draws lessons for ensemble methods, the bias variance tradeoff, calibration, feedback loops and prediction markets.
